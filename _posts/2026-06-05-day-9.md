what_i_learned: |
Today was one of the most exciting days so far because I got a hands-on introduction to computer vision and image recognition. During the session Mr. Daniel reviewed I learned how AI models are trained, tested, and evaluated. We used Teachable Machine to train our own image classification model by giving it hundreds of examples of two different objects. I learned and trained the model to recognize my glasses case and my phone pouch, and after training it with around 200 images each, it was able to correctly identify each object. What amazed me most was seeing the model recognize both objects when they were placed side by side, which helped me better understand how AI learns from examples. I also learned more about how computers "see" images through computer vision. Rather than viewing an image the way humans do, computers break images into tiny units called pixels and look for patterns such as shapes, colors, and edges. We explored concepts like image classification, object detection, object tracking, and image segmentation. I learned that image classification identifies what is in an image, object detection locates objects by drawing boxes around them, and image segmentation goes even further by separating different parts of an image into distinct regions. We also discussed how AI models learn from training examples before being tested on new data to see how accurately they perform. I also learned about OpenCV, a computer vision library used for processing and analyzing images. I learned how it can load images, apply filters, convert them to grayscale, and focus on specific Regions of Interest (ROI) for analysis. We also used NumPy indexing to crop images, which made it easier to zoom in on just the important parts. I got introduced to YOLO, and it was interesting to see how it can identify and track objects in real time. Outside of the technical lessons, I spent time working on my presentation slides for the weekly showcase compiling my week 1 & 2 achievements and challenges which was exciting.
