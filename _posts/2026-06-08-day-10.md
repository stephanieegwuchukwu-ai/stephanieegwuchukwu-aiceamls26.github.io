what_i_learned: |
  Today, we focused on our next major research task: developing a baseline model for ECG classification using a 1D Convolutional Neural Network (1D-CNN). One of the first things I learned was what a 1D-CNN actually is. Unlike image-based models that work in multiple dimensions, a 1D-CNN processes data along a single dimension, in our project would be time. I learned that this type of model can automatically detect important patterns in raw ECG signals without requiring researchers to manually identify features beforehand. It was interesting to see how AI can learn directly from heart signal data and use those patterns to classify different heart conditions. We also spent time going through the model architecture and understanding how the ECG data moves through each layer. I learned about important CNN components such as Conv1D layers, Batch Normalization, ReLU activation functions, Max Pooling, and fully connected layers. One concept that stood out to me was Global Average Pooling (GAP), which summarizes each feature map into a single value instead of flattening everything, helping make the model simpler and more efficient. I also learned how convolutional filters slide across an ECG signal to detect meaningful heartbeat patterns, similar to how someone might look for specific features within a larger signal. Another important part of today's session was understanding the logic behind the data pipeline. Rather than simply running the provided code, we were encouraged to understand what each section was doing and then rewrite it in by ourselves without copying and pasting the code. I learned how detailed ECG diagnosis codes are grouped into five broader categories, making the data easier for the model to work with. I also learned about multi-label encoding, where medical diagnoses are translated into combinations of 0s and 1s so the computer can process them.

blockers: |
 The biggest challenge for me today was understanding the model code while it was being explained. There were a lot of new concepts introduced at once, and I found it difficult to fully grasp the meaning and use of every line during the actual walkthrough by the grad mentor. Because of that, I spent time independently reviewing the code afterward and trying to break it down section by section. After then, at my own pace, I began to understand how the different components work together and how the model processes ECG data. Also, the dataset took a long time to fully download.

reflection: |
 One thing I appreciated about today's research session was that we were expected to understand the reasoning behind each step and eventually reimplement parts of the code ourselves. This pushed me to think more critically about what the model is actually doing rather than just focusing on the final output. I also enjoyed learning how different layers work together to transform raw ECG signals into predictions about heart conditions. By the end of the session, I felt like I had a better understanding of the structure of a baseline 1D-CNN, and I am excited to continue building on that knowledge as our project progresses.
