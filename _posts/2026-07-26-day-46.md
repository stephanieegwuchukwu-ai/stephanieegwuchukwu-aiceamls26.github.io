what_i_learned: |
   Today marked the final day of the SAIRI 2026 Symposium, and it was inspiring to watch every team present the incredible research they had worked on throughout the program. I presented my group’s research, and although I was nervous, I was proud that I stayed confident and delivered our presentation successfully. I also participated in my very first hackathon, where my teammates, Ayomide Aisida, Kosisochukwu Obiora, and I pitched our web-based application, Class Radar, to the K–12 judges. Class Radar is designed to improve classroom engagement by helping teachers monitor real-time student participation while giving students a private way to recognize when they are losing focus and receive interactive prompts, personalized learning activities, and gentle reminders to help them regain attention without feeling embarrassed. The platform also provides teachers with classroom engagement insights, making it easier to identify students who may need additional support while creating a more inclusive and interactive learning environment. Beyond presenting, I visited different research posters and workshops, where I learned about innovative projects spanning artificial intelligence, healthcare, education, and technology. The day ended on an unforgettable note as my graduate mentor won both the People’s Choice Poster Award and the Hackathon, while our team received First Place in the SAIRI 2026 Spotlight Video Competition, making the celebration even more special.
