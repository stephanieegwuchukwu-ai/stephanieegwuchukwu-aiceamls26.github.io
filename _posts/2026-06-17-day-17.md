what_i_learned: |
      Today, I worked on my codes for the model fairness evaluation phase and learned how to run an intersectional fairness audit by checking model performance across age and sex groups. This showed me how models can perform differently across subgroups even when overall accuracy looks good. I learned how the False Negative Rate (FNR) is calculated from a confusion matrix and why it matters in ECG classification. In medical use cases, a high FNR means the model is missing real disease cases, which can lead to serious health risks. Because of this, reducing false negatives is often more important than overall accuracy. I also learned the difference between reusable functions and one-time scripts. Functions are better for scaling and reuse, while scripts are useful for quick testing and analysis. Overall, I gained a better understanding of algorithmic fairness and why it is important to evaluate models across different demographic groups in healthcare.
