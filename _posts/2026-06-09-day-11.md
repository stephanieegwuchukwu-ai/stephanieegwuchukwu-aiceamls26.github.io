what_i_learned: |
  Today was mostly lecture-based, but I learned a lot about research writing and how to use AI as a learning tool. One thing that stood out to me was learning how to make my writing sound like me, even when I'm discussing other people's research. I learned that certain words and phrases can help me show my own perspective instead of simply summarizing what other researchers have said. I also learned that transitions are more important than I realized because they help explain why a point matters and guide readers through my paper. We also talked about how AI should be used to support learning rather than do the thinking for us. One idea I liked was using AI to ask questions that make me think deeper about my writing instead of having it rewrite everything for me. This showed me that AI can be a helpful tool for brainstorming and improving ideas while still allowing me to make my own decisions as a writer. Another thing I learned was how research papers are organized. I now have a better understanding of how a background section moves from introducing a topic to identifying a gap in existing research and explaining why a new study is needed. We also discussed the difference between abstracts that describe expected results and those that report completed findings. Outside of the webinar, I reviewed videos on Object-Oriented Programming (OOP), convolutional neural networks (CNNs), and the differences between 1D and 2D CNNs. I also spent time revisiting the 1D-CNN model and code from our project, and I could also see the progress in my understanding on how the model can actually works.

blockers: |
 A blocker for me today was the amount of information being covered in a relatively short period of time. Since today webinar and lab were lecture-based, I found myself pausing and replaying videos several times to fully understand the explanations and capture important notes from the instructors. While reviewing the research and programming materials, I wanted to make sure I truly understood the concepts rather than just moving through them quickly. This meant spending extra time revisiting the 1D-CNN model and reviewing code that we had already discussed. Although it required patience, I know the repetition has helped strengthen my understanding of the material.

reflection: |
 One thing I really appreciated today was how much more comfortable the mentors makes the labs to feel and I feel that they are doing a great job, by creating an environment where students feel comfortable asking questions, sharing ideas, and supporting each other's learning. Even though today's sessions were more lecture-heavy than usual, I liked seeing how everything connected back to our research project and future writing tasks.
